About BCCO

Making cancer care easier to understand, access and navigate.

A cancer diagnosis changes everything — not just for one person, but for an entire family. In Bihar, too many people face that moment with more questions than answers: Which doctor? What tests? What next? Where to turn?

Bihar Cancer Care Organisation exists for exactly that moment. We turn fear into information, confusion into direction, and isolation into community — so that every family can move forward, one supported step at a time.

  • Awareness, before and after diagnosis
  • Guidance families can trust
  • Navigation, one step at a time
  • Support for the whole family

Know Your Cancer

Understanding is where better care begins.

Simple, reliable information about cancer — what it is, what to watch for, and when to ask a doctor. Written for everyone, in plain language.

Please read first: this page offers general educational information only. It is not medical advice, it does not diagnose anything, and it never replaces consultation with a qualified medical professional. If anything concerns your health, please see a registered doctor.

Basics Types Warning Signs Risk & Prevention Early Detection When to Consult FAQ

The Basics

What cancer actually is.

In plain words

Our bodies are built from cells that grow, divide and replace themselves in an orderly way. Cancer begins when some cells stop following that order — they grow without stopping, and can spread beyond where they began.

Knowing this changes how you face it: cancer is a disease of the body's own discipline, which is why medicine has so many ways to study and treat it. It is not a punishment, not contagious, and never a reason for shame.

Words you'll hear

  • DiagnosisThe process of finding out what is causing a sign or symptom.
  • OncologistA doctor who specialises in the care of people with cancer.
  • ScreeningChecking for possible changes before any symptoms appear.
  • BiopsyTaking a tiny sample of tissue so it can be examined in a laboratory.

Types

Common types of cancer,briefly and calmly.

Each begins in a different part of the body. What matters most is not memorising them — it's knowing that every type deserves a doctor's attention early.

01Chest area

Breast cancer

Begins in breast tissue, most often noticed as a lump or a change in shape or skin. Both women and men can be affected — any persistent change deserves an examination.

02Head & neck

Oral cancer

Occurs on the lips, tongue, cheek lining or throat. Strongly associated with tobacco and areca-nut use — which makes it among the most preventable. Watch for patches, sores or difficulty chewing.

03Reproductive health

Cervical cancer

Affects the cervix and usually develops slowly over years — which makes regular screening especially valuable. HPV infection is a major known factor; ask a doctor about vaccination.

04Chest area

Lung cancer

Begins in the airways or lung tissue. Tobacco smoke is the leading known cause. A cough that stays, breathlessness or chest discomfort should be discussed — especially by smokers.

05Digestive system

Colorectal cancer

Develops in the colon or rectum, usually from small growths called polyps that form slowly over time. Changes in bowel habits, blood in stool or unexplained discomfort are worth reporting early.

06Men's health

Prostate cancer

Occurs in a small gland in men below the bladder, and mostly grows slowly with age. Urinary changes are common with age for many reasons — a doctor can tell which checks make sense.

07Blood & lymph

Blood cancers

Leukaemia and lymphoma affect blood or the lymphatic system — the body's infection-fighting network. Persistent tiredness, fevers, unexplained weight loss or swollen glands lasting weeks should be examined.

08Skin

Skin cancer

The most visible kind — a mole, patch or sore that changes or doesn't heal. Strong sun exposure adds risk for some types; a changing spot is easy to show a doctor early.

Warning Signs

Eight signs wortha conversation.

01Notice

A lump or swelling

Anywhere on the body — breast, neck, armpit, groin — especially one that is new, growing, or painless.

02Notice

Unexplained weight loss

Losing weight noticeably without trying, over weeks, is a signal your body wants examined.

03Notice

A sore that won't heal

Any wound or ulcer — in the mouth or on the skin — that hasn't healed within a few weeks.

04Notice

Unusual bleeding

Bleeding or discharge that isn't normal for you, from any site, deserves a prompt conversation.

05Notice

Bowel or bladder changes

A persistent new pattern — frequency, discomfort or appearance — is worth reporting to a doctor.

06Notice

A cough or hoarseness that stays

Especially beyond a few weeks, or a voice change without a cold to explain it.

07Notice

Difficulty swallowing

Food feeling stuck, or indigestion that doesn't settle, should be checked rather than endured.

08Notice

A mole that changes

In shape, size, colour or sensation — take a photo on your phone and show a doctor.

A sign is a reason to ask — not a reason to fear. Most of these have ordinary causes. The purpose of knowing them is simply this: ask early, and let a qualified doctor answer calmly.

Risk & Prevention

What raises risk —and what lowers it.

Understanding risk

Factors linked with a higher chance of some cancers. Having one doesn't mean cancer will develop — it means it's worth being mindful.

  • Tobacco in any formSmoked or chewed — the single largest known preventable cause.
  • Areca nut & pan masalaStrongly linked to oral cancer, even without tobacco.
  • AlcoholRisk rises with regular, heavy use.
  • Certain infectionsSuch as HPV and hepatitis B — discuss prevention with a doctor.
  • Family historyWorth sharing with a doctor so checks can be planned.

Everyday protection

No habit guarantees prevention — but these meaningfully reduce the risk of several cancers, and improve life regardless.

  • Say no to tobacco & arecaThe most powerful single decision you can make.
  • Limit or avoid alcoholLess is simply better.
  • Move daily, eat simplyFresh, fibre-rich food and regular activity help the whole body.
  • Ask about vaccinationHPV and hepatitis B vaccines exist — a doctor can advise.
  • Keep timely check-upsAnd protect skin from strong sun.

Early Detection & Screening

Found earlier, many cancers are simpler to treat.

With more options, gentler treatment and less disruption to a family's life. That is the whole idea behind early detection — nothing more mysterious than that.

Talk to BCCO about screening

    What it means in practice

  • Screening checks for changes  before symptoms appear.
  • Knowing your normal makes changes easy to notice.
  • Acting on a warning sign early is never overreacting.
  • Encouraging parents and elders to keep check-ups is a gift.

When to Consult

When it's time tosee a qualified doctor.

You don't need certainty to book a consultation — a doubt is reason enough.

A warning sign that lingers

Any sign from the list above that persists beyond two or three weeks — don't wait for it to become urgent.

You use tobacco or areca

A periodic check-up of the mouth and throat is a wise habit — ask a doctor what interval suits you.

Cancer runs in your family

Share the history with a doctor so appropriate checks can be planned calmly, not fearfully.

You've been advised screening

If a test has been recommended and you have questions about it — ask them. Understanding a test helps you complete it.

A diagnosis has entered the family

BCCO can help you prepare questions, understand next steps and find appropriate medical care — alongside your doctor.
